My Benchmark Was 4x Wrong Because of WSL

Launching the server with its working directory on /mnt/c cost ePHPm about 4x on a light workload, and the report built a wrong explanation on top of it. Here's the strace that shows why.

August 20, 2026

8-Minute Read

I do most of my benchmarking on a Windows box in WSL2, because it’s the machine with 32 threads and 62 GB of RAM on my desk. Everybody who does that learns the first rule early: don’t put your data on /mnt/c. DrvFs, the 9p-backed mount that exposes the Windows drives inside WSL, is slow for file-heavy work, so the databases, the docroot and the build output all live on the WSL ext4 disk. My scaling harness for ePHPm even says so in capital letters in its README.
